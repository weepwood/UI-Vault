Design Principles

The Sunset Design Philosophy

Let the gradient breathe, animate slowly, maintain warmth throughout

Let the Gradient Be the Hero

The gradient is the centerpiece, not decoration. All other elements should support and not compete with it. Treat whitespace as atmosphere -- empty areas of gradient create the same expansive feeling as negative space in a landscape photograph.

Follow the Natural Color Order

Gold at the light source, transitioning through orange and pink to purple at the edges. Every gradient should feel like it has a sun behind it, anchoring the composition in a specific direction of warmth.

Animate Sparingly and Slowly

Gradient shifts should feel like watching the sky change, not a strobe light. Keep animation durations above 15 seconds for background shifts, and ensure all motion can be disabled via prefers-reduced-motion.

Maintain Warmth Throughout

Even neutral elements should lean warm rather than cool gray. Balance visual warmth with functional clarity -- the aesthetic should enhance usability, not obscure it. No pure cool grays; tint everything toward peach, mauve, or warm brown.

Radial Sun Glow

A circular radial gradient simulates a sun focal point, creating naturalistic directional warmth.

Animated Gradient Shift

Slowly cycling background-position across an oversized gradient creates a living, breathing sunset canvas.

Noise Grain Texture

SVG fractal noise at low opacity prevents banding and adds the organic film quality of sunset photography.

Warm Glow Orbs

Blurred radial gradients on a dark base create floating light sources reminiscent of distant city lights at dusk.

Horizon Layers
